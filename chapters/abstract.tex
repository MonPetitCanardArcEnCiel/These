    \textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}

This thesis presents a methodological approach for optimizing stealth aircraft shapes, simultaneously integrating aerodynamic performance and radar signature reduction. The objective is to propose an approach adapted to the industrial context, capable of handling complex geometries and meeting the requirements of high-fidelity multi-physics design.

The first part of the work consists of setting up a multi-objective optimization framework, combining parametric modeling of geometry, aerodynamic simulation, and rapid estimation of radar signature. This approach makes it possible to efficiently explore the trade-offs between drag and stealth, identifying a set of Pareto optimal solutions representative of the various possible trade-offs. The use of adjoint methods for calculating gradients is crucial for reducing the number of function evaluations and accelerating the optimization process, making it possible to explore large design spaces. The use of robust geometric modeling, rather than free-form deformation techniques, guarantees the industrial validity of the shapes generated and facilitates the integration of multidisciplinary constraints.

The second part of the work focuses on the application of this approach to optimization using the boundary element method (BEM) in three dimensions, allowing for a more accurate evaluation of the radar signature. A specific adjoint formulation has been developed for the efficient calculation of gradients in this context. Despite certain limitations, particularly related to convergence and the consideration of a single analysis frequency, the approach has significantly reduced the cost function and demonstrated the feasibility of optimizing complex shapes with high fidelity. The results obtained highlight the performance gain compared to conventional methods for calculating the gradient, paving the way for more ambitious optimizations.

In conclusion, this work illustrates the relevance of an integrated digital chain, combining geometric modeling, multi-physics simulation, and advanced optimization, for exploring innovative compromises in aerostealth. The prospects opened up concern the improvement of adjoint formulations, the consideration of multi-frequency cases, the integration of higher fidelity models, and the extension to other disciplines, in order to strengthen the industrial and scientific impact of the proposed approach.
