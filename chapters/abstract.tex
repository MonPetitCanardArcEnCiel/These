    \Large
    \textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}

This thesis presents a methodological approach for optimising stealth aircraft shapes, simultaneously integrating aerodynamic performance and radar signature reduction. The objective is to propose an approach adapted to the industrial context, capable of handling complex geometries and meeting the requirements of high-fidelity multi-physics design.

The first part of the work consists of setting up a multi-objective optimisation framework, combining parametric modelling of the geometry, aerodynamic simulation and rapid estimation of the radar signature. The aerodynamic observables are calculated using a RANS method, and the radar equivalent surface area is calculated using a low-fidelity method called physical optics. In order to improve convergence, we use a multi-frequency approach for calculating the radar cross-section. This approach allows us to efficiently explore the trade-offs between drag and stealth, identifying a set of optimal Pareto solutions representative of the various possible trade-offs. The use of adjoint methods for calculating gradients is crucial for reducing the number of function evaluations and accelerating the optimisation process, making it possible to explore large design spaces. The use of precise geometric modelling, rather than free-form deformation techniques, guarantees the industrial validity of the shapes generated, facilitates the integration of multi-disciplinary constraints and the transition to manufacturing.

The second part of the work focuses on the application of this approach to three-dimensional boundary element method (BEM) optimisation, enabling a more accurate evaluation of the radar signature. A specific adjoint formulation has been developed for the efficient calculation of gradients in this context. Despite certain limitations, particularly related to convergence and the consideration of a single analysis frequency, the approach has significantly reduced the cost function and demonstrated the feasibility of optimising complex shapes with high fidelity. The results obtained highlight the performance gain compared to conventional methods for calculating the gradient, paving the way for more ambitious optimisations.

In conclusion, this work illustrates the relevance of an integrated digital chain, combining geometric modelling, multi-physics simulation and advanced optimisation, for the exploration of innovative compromises in aerodynamic stealth. The prospects opened up concern the improvement of adjoint formulations, the consideration of multi-frequency cases for high-fidelity optimisation, the integration of higher-fidelity models and the extension to other disciplines (structural mechanics, in particular to take into account structural deformations, which impact the RADAR signature, infrared stealth, etc.), in order to strengthen the industrial and scientific impact of the proposed approach.
